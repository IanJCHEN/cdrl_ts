Skin or Structure

Transfer of Traditional Knowledge
AY2020-21 Term 2

In this class ‘weaving,’ one of the architectural origins (Semper) is reevaluated from the tectonic perspective to investigate its design and fabrication opportunities in contemporary architectural creations. There is growing interest in attempting to transform tacit knowledge of traditional handcrafts into explicit knowledge and extend its applications beyond traditional domains with the advancement of contemporary technologies. Highlighting principles of Kagome weaving conventionally used in bamboo/rattan basket making, this course explores design and fabrication methods to transfer the knowledge in architectural production.

Unlike Semper’s textile wall the woven bamboo/rattan basket is a tectonic object that is simultaneously a structure and a skin. The material interlacing holds the shape of the object while creating the weaving pattern as the surface. Questions of tectonics and technology are brought forward while considering the inextricable relationship of the ornament, the skin and the structure that defines one another when applying the principles to architectural scale.

The course is project-based and structure with three parts to raise and answer the questions; conceptualization, designing and making. Through the reviews of literature of related research works and theoretical background, it begins with understanding overview of the interest in such structure and translation of handcraft technologies in the other fields, sorts out key agendas and formulates a framework of a project. The project investigates the complex mechanism that the weaving creates at different scales and explores the intrinsic relationship between material, construction and technology along with the development of the project through design and making workshops. The process of designing and making goes in parallel to mutually inform design and fabrication methods and representational and material procedures. The course will culminate with a production and exhibition of an architectural prototype at the end of the semester.
