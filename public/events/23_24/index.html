Craft and Architecture

Regenerating Hong Kong’s Countryside
AY2023-24 Term 2

This course explores the concept of tectonics, drawing inspiration from the traditional bamboo crafts of the region. The objective is to apply bamboo weaving and other local bamboo craft knowledge to create an architectural structure that contributes to the revitalization of Ng To settlement in Kuk Po village, a rural area in northeast Hong Kong. The project aims to revive the bamboo craft by incorporating it into the design of a new place within the village.

During this course, traditional craft techniques such as bamboo weaving, bamboo scaffolding, and block casting will be examined from a tectonic perspective to explore their potential for use in contemporary architectural creations. The course will focus on reevaluating these techniques to investigate their design and fabrication opportunities in light of advancements in contemporary technologies. There is a growing interest in adapting traditional craft knowledge for use beyond its traditional domains, and this course seeks to explore the possibilities of these techniques in contemporary architecture.

The first part involves identifying problems related to crafts and raising questions. The second part involves conceptualizing a solution to the identified problems. Finally, the third part involves designing and creating a prototype to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed solution. The design project for this course involves designing and constructing a small pavilion that will serve as a shelter along the beautiful rural landscape of Kuk Po River, with the goal of becoming a new node of revitalization for the area, forming the part of ecological and cultural trail.
