Transfer of Traditional Knowledge\
*AY2021-22 Term 2*
<!--more-->

This course explores the notion of tectonic in reevaluating traditional breeze blocks prevalent commonly in modern architecture in tropical and semitropical regions. Breeze block is a simple building material that gives an ambivalent building expression mixed out of functional necessity and aesthetic appeal through repeating a casting block. The course asks for the reinvention of its use in contemporary architecture by careful investigation of relationships of pattern, material, fabrication method, and tools for producing a block. It is a response to the current issue of climate change aiming to create a sustainable built environment through contemporary breeze block design with the latest building technology.

Improving a building’s natural ventilation performance is one strategy to cope with the issue of heat accumulation in a city. Breeze block or ventilation block is a traditional building material, once so popular in the 1950s and 1960s, prompted by the reinvention of concrete technology. The sun-shading and the air-flowing capacity make it suitable for hot and humid climatic conditions, with a construction method that is simple and easy to adopt. The porosity created by repetitive perforated modular units can screen vision and the pattern adds an aesthetic quality. “The return of the breeze block” is notable in recent years, with many contemporary architectural design practices in tropical and subtropical countries paying attention to natural means to improve the living environment with an interest in the expression of local identity. The discussion of the return in breeze block application is linked to a renewed popularity in the study of materiality and their changing role in contemporary architecture, as advanced digital tools revisit old material knowledge in new forms.

The course is project-based to systematically study the material complexity of the concrete block to interrogate its polymorphism, envisioning revival of the design application in contemporary architecture. The course is structured with two parts to raise and answer problems and questions: observation and conceptualization, designing and making. Through studies of existing breeze blocks in the city and reviewing the literature of related work, it begins with understanding the background and current conditions and status of the material to sort out key agendas and formulate a project framework. The project investigates the complex mechanism that a breeze block creates at the intersection of functional requirements and aesthetic interests and explores the intrinsic relationship between material, construction, and technology through design and making in workshops. The process of design and making goes in parallel, mutually informing methods of design and fabrication. The course will culminate with production and exhibition of an architectural prototype at the end of the semester.
