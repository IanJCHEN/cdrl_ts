Tectonics of Folding\
*AY2022-23 Term 2*
<!--more-->

This course explores the notion of tectonic, learning from Origami. It aims at applying folding knowledge to an architectural creation. Origami (Ori means folding, K(g)ami means paper) is a traditional Japanese paper craft, a form of art that transform a flat square sheet of paper into a sculpture by folding techniques. Origami develops fabrication techniques of folding three-dimensional objects out of flat paper without allowing additional tools to cut or mark and connect by pins or glues. Origami enables plain, thin, light, flexible material to gain rigidity and stability by the act of folding.

Defining tectonic as structure and materiality that makes a physical building stand under certain social, political, geographical, and cultural contexts, this course explores the tectonics of folding by observing and making Origami and applying the principles to create architectural spaces beyond the original context of the handcraft.

As Sophia Vyzoviti, the author of Folding Architecture says, "folding is not concerned with creating a new style but rather with searching for links. Form brings up the problem of human scale, as they can unconsciously display monumental characteristics. Working on a larger scale makes this problem visible." Adaptation of the Origami principles and techniques beyond the original scale becomes a challenge to develop new architecture.

The course is project-based to systematically study the relationship between materials, tools, and fabrication methods of folding structures in multiscale, envisioning the design application of traditional Japanese Origami in architectural production. The main design project of the course is a design and making of a folding structure that serves as a stage set for a movement performance. The project will be developed in collaboration with international contemporary dancers from Hong Kong, Rebecca Wong and Addy Chan who will be performing a dance movement with the structure. They will interpret Tale of Genji, a classic work of Japanese literature written in the early 11th century, in their movement performance.
